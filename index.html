The real problem

More reporting can make an organisation feel informed while leaving it unable to decide.

The dashboards grow. The packs become thicker. The analysis becomes more defensible. Yet the conversation still ends with “take that away” or no decision at all. The failure is rarely the data. It is the crossing from evidence to human meaning.

Signal lost

Everything is included, so nothing is emphasised.

The audience has to do the analyst’s work again: find the point, infer the stakes and decide what matters.

Audience missed

The analysis begins where the expert is—not where the decision-maker is.

Technically accurate language fails because it does not connect to the listener’s context, incentives or decision.

Ask missing

The room understands the finding, but nobody knows what should happen next.

A data story is unfinished until it lands on a named choice, owner, trade-off or action.

The working method

The Bridge carries people from a number they can see to a decision they will make.

The near bank

Start where they are.

  • Who is the audience?
  • What decision is genuinely in front of them?
  • Which outcome do they already care about?

The span

Make the meaning travel.

  • Select the signal
  • Build contrast
  • Use an analogy people can repeat
  • Name the human stakes

The far bank

Land on action.

  • What choice must be made?
  • Who must act?
  • What changes if the truth is accepted?

The one-page tool

Audience, decision, signal, stakes, bridge, friction and ask—visible before you build another deck.

Tools from the method

Practical tests for work that has to land.

Y = f(X)

Separate outcomes from levers.

Stop presenting the result as though the audience can directly change it. Show which inputs they can actually influence.

TIME / COST / QUALITY / RISK

Translate into decision language.

The same finding can mean something different through each lens. Use the lens the decision genuinely turns on.

METRIC TEST

Expose measures that create the wrong behaviour.

Ask whether the metric is influenceable, gameable, timely and pointing attention at the right part of the system.

ANALOGY: 4 MOVES

Make difficult evidence repeatable.

Anchor it in something familiar, preserve the important structure, reveal the surprise and return to the decision.
